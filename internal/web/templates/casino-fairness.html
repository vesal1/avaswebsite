How the casino games are decided

A slot machine you cannot check is a machine you have to take on trust. These do not ask you to. Every spin is decided by a seed we commit to before you play and publish afterwards, and the algorithm that turns that seed into reels is written out below so you can implement it yourself and get the same answer.

The commitment

  1. When you first open a game we draw a random 32-byte server seed, and show you only its SHA-256 hash. That hash is the commitment.
  2. You contribute a client seed. Change it whenever you like — it is mixed into every spin from that point on.
  3. Each spin uses the pair plus a counter, the nonce, which starts at 1 and never repeats. Spin 47 is always the 47th nonce.
  4. When you rotate the pair we publish the server seed. You can then hash it, check it against the commitment you were shown before any of those spins happened, and replay every one of them.

The order is what does the work. We cannot look at a spin and change it, cannot pick a seed once we know what you staked, and cannot deal a different result than the seed dictates — any of those would break a commitment that was already in your hands.

The algorithm

The keystream is HMAC-SHA256, keyed with the server seed, over the message clientSeed|nonce| followed by an eight-byte big-endian counter starting at zero. Bytes are taken from each 32-byte block in order, and a new block is generated with the next counter when one runs out.

Each reel's stop is drawn from that stream with rejection sampling: take as many bytes as the strip length needs, discard any value in the ragged tail above the largest whole multiple of the strip length, and take the remainder. It is deliberately not a modulo of a single byte, which would make the first stops on a 55-stop reel slightly likelier than the rest — a small bias, and one that would fall our way.

Reels are drawn left to right. If the spin wins free spins, each free spin draws its reels from the same stream in turn, so the whole round — base spin, feature and all — comes out of one seed.

This is the same generator that shuffles the poker decks. One implementation, audited once.

The return figures

Each machine publishes its return to player. That number is computed, not observed: it is the sum over every outcome the reel strips can produce, weighted by how often they produce it, run through the same code that pays you. Stakes are restricted to levels that divide exactly into the paytable, so no win is ever quietly rounded down and the published figure is the real one rather than a ceiling.

What we do not do

There is no mechanism in this software that varies a machine's odds by who is playing, what they have won, how long they have been at it or what they are holding. The engine that decides a spin is handed a game, a seed and a stake, and is given nothing else — it could not consult your balance if it were asked to. The same applies at the poker tables: the deck is random, and the house takes a capped rake on hands that reach a flop rather than manufacturing hands to rake.
